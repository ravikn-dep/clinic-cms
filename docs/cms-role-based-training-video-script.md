# Clinic CMS Role-Based Training Video Script

**Author:** Manus AI  
**Estimated narration length:** 8 to 10 minutes  
**Audience:** Clinic owner, administrator, reception staff, doctors, pharmacy staff, billing staff, and general clinic users  
**Purpose:** This script is written for a narrated AI training video that teaches the Clinic CMS by role and by module.

> Video style direction: Use a warm, professional clinical tone. The visuals should show a calm dashboard, friendly sidebar navigation, soft clinical colors, and clear cursor guidance. The narrator should speak slowly enough for non-technical clinic staff to follow.

## Scene-by-scene script

| Scene | Approx. duration | Visual direction | Narration |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Welcome | 35 seconds | Show the friendly Clinic CMS dashboard with the sidebar visible. Slowly zoom toward the dashboard summary cards. | Welcome to your Clinic Management System. This training will show you how to publish the system, log in, understand staff roles, and use each major module for patients, consultations, pharmacy inventory, prices, billing, notifications, and audit review. |
| 2. Publishing overview | 55 seconds | Show the Management UI checkpoint card conceptually, then highlight a Publish button area. Do not imply the app is already published unless the owner clicks Publish. | Before staff use the CMS in daily clinic operations, the owner should review the latest checkpoint in the Management UI. Test the preview with sample data, confirm login and permissions, and then click Publish when ready. Publishing is controlled by the owner so the clinic decides exactly when the system goes live. |
| 3. Production readiness | 45 seconds | Display a checklist overlay: test patient, inventory item, bill, admin account, staff account, audit logs. | For full-scale use, run one complete test visit before entering real patient data. Register a sample patient, create a consultation note, add an inventory item, generate an invoice, and check audit logs as an administrator. This dry run confirms that staff understand the process and that role boundaries are correct. |
| 4. Login flow | 55 seconds | Show a login screen or sign-in prompt, then transition to the dashboard after authentication. | Staff begin by opening the published clinic URL and signing in. The system uses authenticated sessions, so staff should use their own account and should not share logins. After signing in, users land on the dashboard, where they can see clinic activity, patient queue information, inventory alerts, and shortcuts into daily work. |
| 5. Roles explained | 75 seconds | Show two role cards: Admin and User. Use checkmarks for allowed actions and locks for restricted actions. | The CMS has two main roles. Admin users are usually owners, clinic administrators, or compliance leads. They can use normal clinic modules, export patient and billing CSV files, and view audit logs. Standard users are staff members such as reception, doctors, pharmacy, or billing staff. They can perform daily clinic work, but they cannot view audit logs or export administrative reports. |
| 6. Giving role access | 70 seconds | Show the Management UI Database panel conceptually and highlight the users table. | To give access, ask a staff member to sign in once. After that, the owner can review the users table from the Management UI database area. Most staff should remain as user. If someone needs administrator access, the owner can change that staff member’s role to admin. Admin access should be limited to trusted staff because it includes exports and audit review. |
| 7. Reception role: patient registration | 70 seconds | Navigate to Patient Registration. Highlight required demographic fields, submit button, QR code, barcode, and print slip. | Reception staff start in Patient Registration. Enter the patient’s first name, last name, date of birth, contact number, and any optional details such as gender, email, or address. After submission, the system creates a patient identifier and tracking assets. The print slip can be used for OPD tracking during the visit. |
| 8. Patient records | 65 seconds | Navigate to Patient Records. Show search, profile tab, consultations, billing records, and stored files. | Patient Records is where staff search and review a patient’s history. The profile shows demographics and identifiers. Consultation history, billing records, QR codes, barcodes, audio references, and invoices are available through protected access. This is the main place to review patient information after registration. |
| 9. Doctor role: Ambient Scribe | 80 seconds | Navigate to Ambient Scribe. Show upload or recording area, transcript, structured note sections, and finalization area. | Doctors and clinical documentation staff use Ambient Scribe to turn consultation audio into structured notes. Upload or capture the audio, review the transcript, then check the structured sections for clinical history, present complaints, advised investigations, and treatment plan. Finalize the note only after clinical review. |
| 10. Pharmacy role: inventory and prices | 85 seconds | Navigate to Pharmacy Inventory. Highlight item name, batch, expiry, quantity, reorder level, and unit price. Show stock cards and low-stock alert styling. | Pharmacy staff manage medicines from Pharmacy Inventory. This is where item name, batch number, expiry date, quantity, reorder level, and unit price are entered. Quantity controls stock availability. Reorder level controls low-stock alerts. Unit price helps staff maintain price information for pharmacy items and billing references. |
| 11. Billing role: invoices and payment | 85 seconds | Navigate to Billing. Show patient ID, line items, quantity, unit price, discount, tax, payment method, and payment status. | Billing staff create invoices in the Billing module. Choose the patient, add consultation or pharmacy items, enter quantity and unit price, then apply discount or tax if needed. After generating the bill, keep payment status current: Pending, Paid, or Partial. Accurate payment status helps the clinic track outstanding collections. |
| 12. Notifications | 45 seconds | Navigate to Notifications. Show unread, read, and operational alerts. | Notifications show operational messages, such as new patient activity, invoice events, or low-stock alerts. Staff can review alerts and mark them as read so the clinic team knows what still needs attention. |
| 13. Admin role: audit logs and exports | 80 seconds | Navigate to Audit Logs as admin. Show filters and activity rows. Then show export controls on records or billing. | Administrators should review audit logs periodically. The audit log records important actions, such as patient creation, PHI access, stock changes, billing activity, and exports. Admins can also export patient or billing CSV data when reporting is required. These capabilities are intentionally restricted from regular users. |
| 14. Editing summary | 80 seconds | Show a split-screen table of modules and edit locations. | Remember where edits happen. Patients are added in Patient Registration and reviewed in Patient Records. Clinical notes are created in Ambient Scribe. Inventory quantity, reorder level, and medicine price are managed in Pharmacy Inventory. Invoice line prices, discounts, tax, and payment status are managed in Billing. Staff roles are managed by the owner in the Management UI users table. |
| 15. Safe operating habits | 60 seconds | Show a calm checklist: separate accounts, test first, use correct module, review audit logs, publish intentionally. | For safe clinic operations, each staff member should use their own login, patient data should be entered carefully, and admins should review access regularly. Before full-scale use, publish only after owner review and run a complete test workflow with sample data. This keeps the system friendly for staff and accountable for clinic management. |
| 16. Closing | 30 seconds | Return to dashboard hero. Show sidebar modules one by one with a final “Ready for clinic review” message. | You are now ready to review, publish, and train staff on the Clinic CMS. Start with a small pilot, confirm each role understands its module, and then move into daily clinic use when the owner is comfortable. |

## Role-specific teaching notes

The training video should treat each role as a separate user journey. Reception needs patient registration and patient search. Doctors need Ambient Scribe and patient history review. Pharmacy staff need inventory, stock levels, reorder alerts, and prices. Billing staff need invoice creation, line-item prices, and payment status. Administrators need role review, exports, audit logs, and production oversight.

| Role | Must learn first | Must avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Owner/admin | Publishing, users table, role changes, audit logs, exports, domain settings. | Giving admin access broadly or publishing before testing with sample data. |
| Reception | Patient Registration, OPD slip printing, Patient Records search. | Editing inventory or billing unless assigned to those duties. |
| Doctor | Ambient Scribe, structured note review, patient consultation history. | Finalizing notes before review. |
| Pharmacy | Medicine stock, expiry, quantity, reorder level, unit price. | Ignoring low-stock thresholds or entering prices inconsistently. |
| Billing | Invoice line items, unit prices, discount, tax, payment method, payment status. | Leaving invoices in Pending when payment has been completed. |
| General staff | Login, dashboard, notifications, basic navigation. | Sharing accounts or accessing patient files without a clinic reason. |

## Voiceover prompt for AI narration

Use a calm, friendly, professional voice. The delivery should sound like a helpful clinic operations trainer rather than a software engineer. Avoid rushing. Emphasize the words **admin**, **user**, **Patient Registration**, **Patient Records**, **Ambient Scribe**, **Pharmacy Inventory**, **Billing**, **Notifications**, and **Audit Logs** when those modules are introduced.

## Visual generation prompt for AI video

Create a professional training video for a friendly Clinic Management System. Show a warm clinical dashboard interface with soft teal, warm ivory, mint, and gentle coral accents. Include sidebar navigation, login flow, role cards for Admin and User, patient registration form, patient records search, ambient scribe note sections, pharmacy inventory table, billing invoice editor, notifications list, and audit log review. Use clean screen-recording style motion, subtle cursor highlights, readable labels, gentle zooms, and calm healthcare-office background visuals. The video should feel reassuring, modern, and easy for non-technical clinic staff to follow.
